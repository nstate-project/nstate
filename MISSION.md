# nstate — Mission

## The shortest version

> **nstate shows you exactly how governments waste your money — and builds the open-source tools to fix it. The end goal: a minimum viable state that costs less, does more, and leaves you alone.**

---

## The problem

Your government is too big, too expensive, and too comfortable with failure.

Not because politicians are uniquely evil. Because the system is designed to grow. Every crisis becomes a programme. Every programme gets a department. Every department fights for its budget. Nobody measures whether it worked. Nobody loses their job when it doesn't. And the people who pay for all of it — you — have never been given an honest, traceable, reproducible account of what they got in return.

This is not bad luck. Opacity is politically useful. It is almost impossible to cut something nobody has measured.

Meanwhile: taxes are at generational highs in the UK. Public services are in crisis. Trust in government is in freefall. Voting rates are at historic lows. People can feel that something is deeply wrong — but they don't have the tools to say exactly what, prove it, and force the argument.

nstate is those tools.

---

## What we do

We take government spending — the actual data, published by the government itself — and we run it through a simple, brutal filter:

1. **What was this supposed to do?** (stated objective, in the government's own words)
2. **What did it actually do?** (measured outcomes, official data, before and after)
3. **What did it cost?** (full cost — receipts, admin, downstream effects, who paid)
4. **Is there a cheaper way?** (open-source alternatives, benchmarked against the current system)
5. **If a machine can do it better — why are humans doing it?**

Every finding is reproducible. Every number traces to a source. Every pipeline is on GitHub. If we're wrong, show us — and we'll correct it publicly.

---

## The goal: minimum viable state

Not zero government. Courts, property rights, contract enforcement, public health infrastructure — these are legitimate and necessary. We are not anarchists.

But there is an enormous distance between "legitimate state functions" and where we are now. The question nstate asks — every day, for every programme — is: **does this need to exist, in this form, at this cost?**

Our goal is to work through that question systematically, across every domain of public spending, until the case for the current size of the state is impossible to make without engaging our evidence.

Then we build the minimum viable alternative — open-source, auditable, deployable by any government that wants it.

Most won't. That refusal becomes part of the record too.

---

## The sequence

**Phase 1 — Show the waste.** Policy-by-policy scorecards. Same template, same evidence standards, same methodology for everything. UK first. The government's own data, turned into a clear verdict: achieved / partially achieved / failed. Cost per outcome. Who paid. Who benefited.

**Phase 2 — Build the fix.** For every audited administrative process, an open-source agent that can do it cheaper. We shadow-run it alongside the current system and publish the comparison. Any government can adopt it. Open-source, free, documented.

**Phase 3 — Scale the movement.** A public platform where citizens see what their money is actually doing — simply, specifically, shareable. Country chapters that fork the methodology and run the same audit for their own governments. Contributors going deep on individual policy areas. A growing evidence base that compounds over time.

---

## Why now

Governments are not going to audit themselves. Think tanks are fragmented, slow, and often captured by their funders. The media covers the politics, not the evidence.

But the data exists. It is public. And modern AI can process, analyse and surface it at a scale no previous generation of reformers had access to.

The moment for this is now. The tools exist. The anger exists. The data exists.

All that was missing was the commitment to do it rigorously, openly, and continuously — until the evidence becomes impossible to ignore.

That is nstate.

---

## What we are not

- Not a political party or campaign
- Not affiliated with any funder, think tank, or government
- Not ideologically pre-committed to any finding — if a programme works well at reasonable cost, we say so
- Not interested in individual politicians — we measure systems, not people
- Not theoretical — every claim is grounded in data you can download and rerun

---

## The standard of proof

> **Show the evidence, the beneficiaries, the alternatives and the opportunity cost — or stop claiming the spending is justified.**

That is the bar we set for governments. It is also the bar we set for ourselves.

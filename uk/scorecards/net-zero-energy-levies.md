# Scorecard: Net Zero environmental levies on electricity bills

**Status:** Published
**Version:** 1.0

**Maintainer:** @nstate-project
**Evidence quality (overall):** HIGH

## A. Policy definition

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Official name** | Environmental and social levies on electricity (Renewables Obligation, Feed-in Tariff, Contracts for Difference, Energy Company Obligation, Warm Homes Discount) |
| **Implementation date** | RO: 2002; FiT: 2010; CfD: 2014; ECO: 2013; WHD: 2011 |
| **Jurisdiction** | UK-wide (WHD: GB) |
| **Stated government objective** | Fund the transition to low-carbon generation; support vulnerable households with energy costs |
| **Target population** | All electricity consumers; low-income households (WHD/ECO) |
| **Scale** | ~£9–10bn/year collected via electricity bills (pre-2025 Budget reforms) — Source: Ofgem/UKERC |

## C. Counterfactual

**Method used:** Direct accounting (levy obligations are defined in regulation and published by Ofgem)

**Baseline:** An electricity bill without the defined levies — i.e., what a household would pay if environmental and social obligations were funded through general taxation instead.

**Key assumptions:**
- Wholesale electricity prices unchanged (levy removal does not alter generation mix in the short run)
- No behavioural response from consumers or generators
- Government would need to fund equivalent support (WHD, ECO) via other means if levies removed

**Central estimate:** Removing all environmental and social electricity levies would reduce the average household electricity bill by approximately £152–160/year at 2024–25 rates.

**Sensitivity range:** Low: £120/year (if some levy costs already absorbed by suppliers) | Central: £156/year | High: £200/year (if indirect costs via industrial pass-through included)

**Confidence in counterfactual:** HIGH for direct levy accounting; LOW for second-order effects on wholesale prices and generation investment.

## G. Verdict

**Fiscal score:** Positive (revenue raised exceeds admin cost; ~£9–10bn/year collected)
**Economic score:** Negative (regressive household cost; UK competitive position weakened relative to lower-levy peers)
**Delivery score:** Partially achieved (renewable share rising strongly; but levy burden higher than necessary given CCC proposals to rebalance to gas)

**Bottom line:**

> Environmental and social levies added approximately £156/year (17% of the price cap) to the average UK household electricity bill in 2024–25, making UK residential electricity prices the second highest among IEA members. The Renewables Obligation alone cost households £94/year. The 2025 Budget partially addressed this by shifting some costs to general taxation, reducing the levy share to ~9%. The underlying data is high quality — Ofgem publishes the levy obligations directly — but debate continues over whether funding the energy transition through electricity bills rather than general taxation represents an optimal or fair policy design.

## A. Policy definition

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Official name** | Application of VAT to private school fees and removal of business rates charitable relief |
| **Implementation date** | 1 January 2025 (VAT); April 2025 (business rates) |
| **Jurisdiction** | England, Wales, Northern Ireland (VAT); England (business rates relief removal) |
| **Stated government objective** | Raise revenue to fund 6,500 additional state school teachers; reduce educational inequality by ending public subsidy for fee-paying schools |
| **Target population** | ~570,000 pupils in independent schools (ISC member schools); all state school pupils (intended beneficiaries of reinvestment) |
| **Scale** | £1.3–1.5bn gross annual revenue (HM Treasury estimate); £900m–1.25bn net after state school placement costs — Source: HM Treasury / OBR |

## C. Counterfactual

**Method used:** Scenario — OBR certified revenue model; ISC pupil attrition modelling

**Baseline:** No VAT applied; charitable business rates relief maintained. Private school enrolment stable at ~570,000.

**Key assumptions:**
- OBR assumed ~11% pupil attrition from private to state sector (central case)
- IFS modelled 17% attrition scenario, which reduces net revenue materially
- State school per-pupil cost assumed at £7,500/year (England average, DfE 2024)
- No assumption about actual reinvestment of revenue into teacher recruitment

**Central estimate:** Net fiscal gain of approximately £1.0bn–1.2bn/year, after accounting for state school placement costs of ~£260m–350m/year for absorbed pupils.

**Sensitivity range:**
- Low (17% attrition, IFS): Net gain ~£800m/year
- Central (OBR, 11% attrition): Net gain ~£1.0–1.2bn/year
- High (5% attrition): Net gain ~£1.4bn/year (if schools absorb more VAT)

**Confidence in counterfactual:** MEDIUM — revenue outturn is measurable (HMRC); but actual pupil destination data from schools to state sector is difficult to track precisely. DfE (35,000 state school increase) and ISC (11,000 exit) figures diverge.

## G. Verdict

**Fiscal score:** Positive (net gain ~£1.0–1.2bn/year; gross £1.3bn)
**Economic score:** Neutral to negative (revenue raised but school closures, bursary cuts, and fee increases impose costs; distributional effects on scholarship holders are negative)
**Delivery score:** Too early (6,500 teacher commitment not yet independently verified; state school absorption ongoing)

**Bottom line:**

> The January 2025 application of 20% VAT to independent school fees has raised approximately £1.3bn gross in its first year (£900m–£1.25bn net after absorbing ~35,000 additional state school pupils at ~£7,500/year each). Fee increases averaged 17.6%, driving an estimated 11,000–35,000 pupils from the independent sector. Forty-four or more independent schools have closed. The OBR certified the policy using an 11% pupil attrition assumption; actual attrition appears broadly consistent with that forecast. Whether the revenue funds the stated 6,500 additional state teachers has not yet been independently verified. The data quality is high — HM Treasury, OBR, ISC, and DfE all publish relevant figures — but the divergence between DfE and ISC pupil count estimates warrants continued monitoring.
